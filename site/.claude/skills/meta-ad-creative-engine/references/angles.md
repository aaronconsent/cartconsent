# Angle library

An "angle" is the belief you're attacking, not the wording. A good batch spreads across angles so
the test tells you *what argument* moves this trade — then Mode 3 drills into the winner. Every
example hook below is already compliant (no phone, no covert tracking, no guarantees); still
re-check against `compliance.md` after you adapt them.

Each angle has: the core belief it targets, why it works, and example hooks. Hooks are the
*primary-text opener* (first ~125 chars) unless marked as a headline.

---

## 1. Wasted ad spend

**Targets:** "I already pay for traffic (Google, LSA, yard signs) — why isn't it converting?"
**Why it works:** contractors feel the leak; they just can't see it. Frame it as *their own*
traffic they've already bought.

- "You're paying for clicks. ~98% of them leave your site without a trace — and you never find
  out who they were."
- "The homeowners who visit your site and don't call aren't gone. You just can't see them yet."
- Headline: "Stop paying for traffic that ghosts you"

## 2. Competitor envy / shared-lead resentment

**Targets:** "Thumbtack/Angi/HomeAdvisor sell me the same lead as 4 other guys."
**Why it works:** near-universal contractor grievance. We're the anti-reseller.

- "That $80 Angi lead? Four of your competitors got it too. Ours are yours alone — $7, never
  resold."
- "Tired of racing three other shops to call the same lead first? Own yours instead."
- Headline: "One lead. One contractor. $7."

## 3. ROI per booked job

**Targets:** "Is this worth it?" — the math-brain owner.
**Why it works:** a single booked job dwarfs the lead cost. Let *them* plug in the numbers;
never promise the outcome.

- "A booked HVAC install is worth thousands. A lead is $7. You don't need many to win."
- "Do the math on your average ticket vs a $7 lead. That's the whole pitch."
- Headline: "One booked job pays for 500 leads"

## 4. Consent / trust vs shady data brokers

**Targets:** "Isn't this creepy / risky?" and the pride angle — "I run a clean business."
**Why it works:** it's our moat. Consent-first is the reason this is safe *and* different.

- "Every lead opted in. Nobody's identified until they say yes. Clean, consent-first, yours."
- "Not scraped. Not resold. Not sketchy. Just the visitors who raised their hand on your site."
- Headline: "Leads that opted in. Not scraped."

## 5. Speed-to-lead

**Targets:** "The first to follow up wins the job."
**Why it works:** contractors know response time decides who books it. (Keep it email-follow-up,
never phone/dialer claims.)

- "The visitor who left your site 10 minutes ago is still deciding. Email them back before your
  competitor does."
- "Follow up while it's hot — real name, real email, the moment they leave your site."
- Headline: "Reach them back while they're still deciding"

## 6. Ownership / build-your-own-list

**Targets:** "I'm renting my pipeline from a platform."
**Why it works:** speaks to owners who want an asset, not a subscription treadmill.

- "Stop renting leads from a platform. Turn your own website traffic into a list you own."
- "Your traffic. Your leads. Your list. Not the platform's."

## Per-trade flavor

Same angles, tuned to the trade's economics and language. Use the trade's real ticket sizes and
seas: the more specific, the more it lands. (Ticket ranges are illustrative — have the contractor
confirm; never state a guaranteed return.)

- **HVAC** — high-ticket installs, seasonal urgency (heat waves / first freeze). ROI-per-job and
  speed-to-lead hit hardest. "It's 98° and the phone's not ringing — but people are on your site."
- **Roofing** — storm-driven demand, big tickets, heavy Angi/HomeAdvisor spend. Competitor-envy
  and wasted-spend angles land. "After a storm, everyone's Googling roofers. Catch the ones who
  land on *you*."
- **Plumbing** — mix of emergency + planned, lower avg ticket but volume. Speed-to-lead + wasted
  spend. "A burst pipe doesn't wait — but not everyone calls on the first visit."
- **Electrical / remodeling / solar / etc.** — longer consideration, higher ticket. ROI-math and
  consent/trust (bigger purchase = more trust needed).
- **General fallback** for the other trades: lead with wasted-spend (universal) + ROI-math, add
  the competitor angle if they buy shared leads.

Consent Resolve covers 17 trades total. If a trade isn't listed, pick the two angles above that
fit its ticket size and urgency profile, and mirror the language of that trade's landing page at
`consentresolve.com/[trade]-leads/`.

## Building the spread (quick recipe)

For a 5-variant batch, a reliable default spread:
1. Wasted ad spend (the universal opener)
2. Competitor envy (if they buy shared leads) OR ROI-math (if not)
3. Consent/trust (the differentiator)
4. Speed-to-lead
5. A per-trade-flavored version of whichever of the above fits the trade best

That's five genuinely different arguments — a real test, not five paint jobs on one idea.
