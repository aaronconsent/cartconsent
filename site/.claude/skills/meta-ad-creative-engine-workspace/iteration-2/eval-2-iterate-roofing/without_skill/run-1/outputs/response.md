## Which Is Winning

**Winner: Creative C (ROI-math).** It's the only creative under your $30 target ($27 CPL) and it has the highest lead-conversion rate (5.3% of clickers become leads). It gets fewer clicks but the clicks it gets are far more qualified — people who engage with hard ROI numbers are further down the buying path. That's exactly what you want for a considered purchase like a roof.

**Runner-up: Creative A (wasted-spend).** At $31 CPL it's a hair over target but genuinely healthy — the strongest CTR (2.56%) and a solid 4.3% lead rate. It's pulling the most volume (9 leads) and doing the top-of-funnel work of getting clicks. Don't kill it; it's your volume engine and it's basically at target.

**Loser: Creative B (competitor-envy).** $58 CPL is nearly 2x your target. Same impressions and clicks as A, but only 4 leads — the lowest CVR (2.2%). The competitor-envy angle is attracting clicks that don't convert. **Turn it off now** and reallocate its ~$33/day.

---

## What's Really Going On

1. **C's frequency is a red flag.** At 3.4 (vs. ~2.0 for A and B) the same people are seeing C too often on a smaller audience. It's winning now, but frequency north of 3 for a week means fatigue is coming — CPL will climb if you don't refresh or widen the audience. Its efficiency is real, but its runway is short.

2. **C's low CTR isn't a bug, it's the angle.** ROI-math self-selects. Fewer people click, but the ones who do are serious. That's why its CVR is highest. The square ratio may also be limiting reach/placement efficiency vs. the 4x5s.

3. **A vs. C is a classic volume-vs-efficiency split.** A casts a wide net (high CTR, decent conversion). C runs narrow and deep (low CTR, high conversion, low CPL). You want both working — they're doing different jobs.

4. **B's problem is the message, not the mechanics.** Delivery is fine (impressions/clicks match A). The competitor-envy hook draws curiosity clicks that don't have buying intent for roofing. Dead angle — don't rebuild it.

## Recommended Actions This Week

**Budget:**
- Kill B. Reallocate its spend split ~60/40 toward A and C.
- Give C a slightly wider or fresh audience to pull frequency back under 3 before scaling budget into it.

**Keep running:** A and C.

## What to Make Next

Make **two** new creatives to test against the winners:

### Next Creative #1 — "ROI-math, but scroll-stopping" (primary bet)
Take C's winning ROI-math angle and fix its two weaknesses (low CTR + square ratio + fatigue):
- **Ratio:** 4x5 (matches your higher-CTR format, gets more placements than square).
- **Hook:** Lead with the ROI payoff in the first 3 words / first frame so it stops the scroll the way A does, then deliver the math. e.g. *"Your roof could be costing you $XXX/month"* → then the numbers.
- **Goal:** keep C's 5%+ conversion while lifting CTR toward A's 2.5%. If it works, this becomes your new top performer.

### Next Creative #2 — "Wasted-spend + a hard number" (volume variant)
Take A's high-CTR wasted-spend angle and inject C's ROI specificity to push CPL under $30:
- **Ratio:** 4x5.
- **Concept:** Same "you're wasting money" emotional hook that earns A's clicks, but close with a concrete dollar figure / savings calc (borrowed from C) to lift the lead rate from 4.3% toward 5%+.
- **Goal:** keep A's click volume but drop CPL from $31 to sub-$30.

### The theme
Both winners revolve around **money made concrete** (wasted spend, ROI). The competitor-envy/emotional-comparison lane is dead. Your next round should double down on specific-dollar-figure creatives and drop anything that trades on comparison or curiosity.

**Test structure:** Run #1 and #2 as a fresh ad against the still-live A and C. Give each ~50 leads or ~$150–200 before judging (18 leads/week total means you need ~2 weeks of data per creative to trust a CPL — current per-creative lead counts of 4–9 are too small to be statistically firm, so treat this week's read as directional, not final).
