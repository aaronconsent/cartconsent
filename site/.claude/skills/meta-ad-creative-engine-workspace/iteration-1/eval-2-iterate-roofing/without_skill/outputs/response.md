Target CPL: **$30**.

## What's actually winning

**Creative A is the clear winner, and Creative C is a sleeper.** Here's the honest read:

- **A (wasted-spend)** is the workhorse. It has the best CTR by a wide margin (2.56%), pulls the most leads in absolute terms (9), and lands at $31 CPL — a dollar over target, which is a rounding error. It's healthy: freq is only 2.1, so it has room to keep spending before the audience burns out. This is your control.

- **C (ROI-math)** is deceptively good and deceptively bad at the same time. Its $27 CPL is technically the *best of the three* and the only one under target. But look at *why*: it barely gets clicked (1.00% CTR — less than half of A's), yet the people who do click convert at the highest rate (5.3%). That's a classic "high-intent, low-reach" pattern — the ad filters hard, so only serious buyers click, but you're leaving volume on the table. The real problem is **frequency 3.4**. That ad is being shown to the same people over and over, which is inflating impressions and starving reach. The low CTR is at least partly fatigue, not a bad message. Don't kill C — the ROI-math *angle* is converting. Refresh the *creative*.

- **B (competitor-envy) is the loser. Kill it.** $58 CPL is nearly 2x target, worst CTR-to-lead efficiency, and no redeeming signal (freq is fine at 2.0, so it's not a fatigue excuse — the message just isn't landing). Cut it and reallocate the budget.

### One caveat on the sample size
These are small lead counts (4–9 leads each). A 4-vs-9 lead gap between B and A is directionally trustworthy, but the C-vs-A CPL difference ($27 vs $31) is well inside the noise band — do **not** crown C the winner on $27 alone. Read A as the volume winner and C as a promising angle that needs a fresh execution and reach fix.

## Decisions for this week

1. **Kill B.** Reallocate its budget to A.
2. **Scale A.** It's under-fatigued and near target. Push more budget here; watch that freq stays under ~3.
3. **Refresh C's creative, keep C's angle.** Freq 3.4 says the *asset* is worn out, not the *message*. New visual, same ROI-math hook. Also test C's angle in **4x5** — both winners (A) and near-winners live in 4x5; C being square may be costing it placement reach (square gets squeezed in Reels/Stories).

## Next creative batch (build these 4)

The theme that's working is **loss/waste framing + concrete numbers**. A ("wasted spend") won on CTR; C ("ROI math") won on conversion quality. The next batch should cross-breed those two winners and abandon the competitor-envy lane entirely.

**All in 4x5** (the format both survivors favor; retire square as the primary).

1. **A-variant "Wasted spend, harder"** — same winning angle, new hook. Lead with a specific dollar figure of money lost to a bad/delayed roof ("The average homeowner wastes $X waiting on a roof that's already failing"). Keep A's proven structure; just swap the visual and sharpen the number.

2. **A × C hybrid "The math on waiting"** — take A's wasted-spend emotion and bolt on C's ROI-math proof. Show the cost of *not* acting now vs. the cost of the repair, side by side. This is the highest-upside test: it pairs the best-CTR angle with the best-CVR angle.

3. **C-refresh in 4x5** — the ROI-math angle with a brand-new asset and the format change. Direct swap-in for the fatigued square C. Same copy skeleton, fresh design.

4. **New-angle seed: "before it's an emergency"** — one exploratory swing so the batch isn't all iteration. Urgency/consequence framing (small leak now vs. deck replacement later). Keeps a loss frame but tests a fresh emotional trigger to find the next A.

**Batch logic:** 2 iterations on the proven winner (A + A/C hybrid), 1 refresh of the fatiguing performer (C), 1 true exploration. That's a 3:1 exploit-to-explore ratio — aggressive enough to keep improving CPL, safe enough not to torch the week on unproven swings.

## What to measure next week
- Did the A/C hybrid beat A's CTR *and* C's CVR? If yes, that's your new control.
- Did refreshed-C's freq drop and CTR recover? Confirms the fatigue diagnosis.
- Keep the target line at $30 CPL and hold each new creative to a ~$40 kill threshold after ~5+ leads (don't kill on 1–2).
